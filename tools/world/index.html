🌍 Where Am I in the World?

Global wealth is distributed so unevenly that almost everyone misjudges their own place in it — usually downwards. This puts one number on the published distribution and tells you where it lands. It is a fact about a dataset, not a verdict about you, and the difference matters enough that half this page is about what the number leaves out.

What are you worth?

i

Everything you own, minus everything you owe. Property, pensions, investments, savings and the car — less the mortgage, the loans and the card balances. Not your salary: this is a stock, not a flow. A negative figure is a real answer, and the tool handles it.

The source measures wealth per adult, so a couple's assets are conventionally split. Ticking this halves the figure before ranking it.

The source

Bands come from the UBS Global Wealth Report, which continues the Credit Suisse series. The four published tiers cover — adults and about — of household wealth.

Above USD 1 million the curve is a power law fitted to two published counts — roughly 60 million millionaires and 3,000 billionaires. It was checked against two figures it was not built from, predicting about 2.2 million adults over USD 10 million and 81,000 over USD 100 million, against reported values of roughly 2.2–2.5 million and 80,000.

      What this number is not

        Net worth is converted at market exchange rates, not purchasing power, which is the convention for wealth and which overstates the gap in living-standard terms: a given sum buys far more in Lagos than in Oslo. The distribution mixes every age together, so a twenty-five-year-old and a sixty-five-year-old with the same balance sheet are treated identically when in reality they are nothing alike. The lower bands are interpolated across a range the source does not break down further. This is a description of a dataset, not financial advice and not a judgement, and nothing you type leaves your device.
